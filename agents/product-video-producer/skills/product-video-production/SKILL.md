# Product Video Production

Choose whatever way makes the best video with the least effort. You can edit the user's footage, animate screenshots, draw screens from the source code, record the running product, use slides, or mix these. Let the material and the goal decide the way, not habit.

What earlier videos taught:

- **Check the story first.** Before a long production, show the user the story and the script in a few lines. A short question saves a whole redo.
- **Show the real power.** Find what makes the feature worth watching. A correct video built on the smallest example that works was rejected as boring.
- **Nothing invented.** Screens you draw or rebuild show what the product really does and how it looks today. Never invent features, results or data.
- **Running the product when it helps.** Sometimes the best material is the product itself: a real run to record, or screens the material lacks. Then start an isolated test instance with the project's own tool, which its testing guide or skills describe. Drive and record it with `browser-automation`, and stop it when you are done. Never use the user's own running app, data, accounts or browser.
- **Project settings.** Follow the voice, pronunciation and brand settings the project or its video folder has (for example, a `VOICE-SETTINGS.md`).
- **Looks the user rejected:** a dimmed or covered product, near-black or near-white backgrounds, zooms that cut off text, captions that differ from the voice, "energetic" voice styles, and version numbers in the video.
- **Headless Chromium** cannot play H.264, so convert clips you embed in HTML scenes to WebM (VP9).

Deliver the video files with a short note: what you made, from which material, and anything still open.
