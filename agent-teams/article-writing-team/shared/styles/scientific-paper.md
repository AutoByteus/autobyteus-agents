# Scientific Paper

The form researchers publish in, for readers who judge claims by method and evidence. A formal report is the same without related work and contributions: scope, method, observations, limitations, bounded conclusions.

- **Reader:** researchers and practitioners of the field.
- **Opening:** the title states the result; the abstract (up to about 250 words) gives the problem, the approach, the main result with its number, and its limit.
- **Order:** the parts a paper has:
  1. Introduction: the problem, why it matters, the gap in earlier work, the contributions as a short list.
  2. Related work: each earlier work credited only with what it says, and how this work differs.
  3. Method: enough to repeat it (setup, data, procedure, measures).
  4. Results: what was measured, each number with its conditions and sample size; figures and tables numbered, captioned and referred to in the text.
  5. Discussion and limitations: what the results mean, the threats to their validity, what they do not show.
  6. Conclusion: only what the results support.
  7. References.
- **Citations:** each outside claim cites its source where it is used; each reference gives authors, title, venue or publisher, year and a link; each cited work was read beyond its abstract, or the reference says what was read (principles, section 2).
- **Sentences:** formal and low in rhetoric; `we` for the authors; numbers with units and uncertainty; no claim beyond the results.
- **The page:** restrained: one column, numbered figures and tables with captions, the references at the end; figures of the method and the results, no decoration, motion only where it shows a process the results depend on.
- **Review:** each contribution maps to a result, each result to its method, and each spot-checked citation to the source.
