# Professional

For practitioners who already hold the field's concepts: they get the key statements at once, with the reasons, and the figures carry the structure. Example: an article for engineering teams that already use coding agents, on how to build a scalable software factory with agent teams.

- **Reader:** practitioners; `brief.md` lists the concepts they hold, and the article does not explain them.
- **Opening:** the point, or the few key statements it rests on, as a short list, each then explained in its own paragraph (principles, the foundation: an expert reader can place every word of a summary).
- **Order:** by the main ideas, each named in the author's own terms and given with the reason it holds; the ending states the conclusion and what is still open, in a few lines.
- **Sentences:** concise. The information check (principles, section 5) is strict: no lead-ins, announcements or framing lines; an example only where it makes an idea clear, told in one or two sentences.
- **The page:** the key statements and numbers stand out in the text's flow; each structure the article names (roles, handoffs, a flow, a ladder of evidence) has a figure where the text needs it, and the prose around a figure does not repeat it.
- **Review:** a practitioner who reads it once can state each main idea and its reason.
