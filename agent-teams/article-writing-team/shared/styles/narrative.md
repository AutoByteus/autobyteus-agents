# Narrative

A story, only when the user asks for one: fiction (a novel, a short story) or narrative non-fiction (what happened, told as a story). [Explanatory writing](../explanatory-writing.md) does not apply; the writing principles (the foundation, grounding) and [figures and the page](../figures-and-page.md) do.

- **Reader:** as the brief says; they can place every name, place and term when it appears (principles, the foundation).
- **Opening:** a scene: someone, somewhere, wanting something.
- **Order:** scenes in time order or a deliberate frame: a character who wants something, what stands in the way, what they do, and what changes; the ending resolves the change. One point of view per scene. The outline holds the premise, the main character and what they want, the scenes in order (what happens, what changes) and the ending, instead of the point and the section chain.
- **Sentences:** show in action, detail and dialogue rather than summary; a sentence may carry mood and rhythm.
- **Truth:** non-fiction keeps every fact grounded (principles, section 1), and invented dialogue or thought is marked or left out. Fiction says it is fiction and states nothing false about real people or products as true.
- **The page:** book typography: a comfortable line length and line height, scene or chapter breaks, illustrations rather than diagrams, motion only when asked.
- **Review:** the reader always knows who and where, each scene moves the story, and the change at the end is clear.
