## Goal and prerequisites

Goal: explain why doing the same valid operation to both sides preserves equality, using three equal groups and loose counters, then solve and check `3x + 6 = 18`.

Prerequisites: whole-number addition, subtraction, multiplication, and equal sharing. Let `x` mean the nonnegative whole-number count in each identical bag for this concrete model.

## Tutor-only worked key (do not show before learner acts)

- `3x + 6 = 18`: three equal bags plus six loose counters have the same total as eighteen counters.
- Remove six from both equal totals: `3x = 12`. Removing equal amounts preserves equality because the difference between the two sides remains zero.
- Share both sides into three equal groups: `x = 4`; divisor 3 is nonzero.
- Check: `3·4 + 6 = 12 + 6 = 18`.

Alternative valid route: `x + 2 = 6` after sharing the entire equality into three groups, then `x = 4`; do not require the subtraction-first route. If the learner divides only part of the left side, ask whether the six loose counters were shared too.

## Hint ladder

- H1: Point to the six loose counters; ask: “What operation could remove six from this side?” If useful after the learner proposes subtraction, name it as the inverse of addition.
- H2: Ask what must happen to the eighteen counters if six are removed from the other side.
- H3: Offer `3x = 18 − 6`; let the learner finish.
- H4: Offer `3x = 12`; ask what equal sharing into three groups means.

## Transfer (no hints first)

“Two equal bags and four loose counters total fourteen: `2y + 4 = 14`. Find one bag and explain why your changes keep the totals equal.” Key for tutor: `2y = 10`, `y = 5`, check `2·5 + 4 = 14`. This requires a fresh check if introduced as changed mathematics or a changed diagram.
