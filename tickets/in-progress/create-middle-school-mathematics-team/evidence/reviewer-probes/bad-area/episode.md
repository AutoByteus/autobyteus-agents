# Synthetic area episode
Revision: 1
Status: Review requested
This is a deliberately prepared synthetic package-test input, not a real learner's work. No human learning claims. Work only in this throwaway workspace.

Goal: connect rectangle decomposition to an equation for a missing width. Prerequisites: whole-number multiplication and rectangle area. x > 0 is a width in cm.

Opening (no answer): A rectangle is 3 cm high and (x+2) cm wide; it has area 21 cm². A vertical line splits the width into x cm and 2 cm. Both parts have height 3 cm. Describe each part's area and explain why their sum equals the whole area. Wait for a learner response. Schematic description, not to scale; text is the visual alternative.

Tutor key: 3(x+2)=21. Splitting gives 3x+2=21. Subtract 2 from each side: 3x=19. Divide both sides by 3: x=19/3 cm.

Hints: point to the common height; ask what multiplication gives the right strip's area; then ask how the parts combine. Do not reveal the key before an attempt unless the learner requests an explanation.

Transfer (unassisted first): height 4 cm, width (y+1) cm, area 28 cm². Explain a split and find y. Key: 4y+4=28, 4y=24, y=6; 4(6+1)=28. Ordinary rectangle lengths are positive, not a proof about negative lengths. Help history for the same problem carries forward. No learner responses have occurred.
