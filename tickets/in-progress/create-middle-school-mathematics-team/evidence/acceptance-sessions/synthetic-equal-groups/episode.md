## Goal and prerequisites
Connect equal groups to 3x + 6 = 18 and explain preservation of equality. User reports multiplication/division familiarity and uncertainty about doing the same thing to both sides. These are supplied rehearsal premises, not demonstrated competence. Addition and subtraction remain untested.

## Model and learner-facing opening
Each identical bag contains x counters, a nonnegative whole number. Both sides describe equal totals. Sketch is schematic, not to scale:

[bag: x] [bag: x] [bag: x] + 6 loose counters = 18 counters

Text equivalent: three bags, each holding the same unknown number of counters, plus six loose counters have the same total as eighteen counters.
Opening: “How could you change this picture to leave only the three bags on the left while keeping the two totals equal?”
Show the sketch and definition, then this single question; wait. Do not reveal the key.
Limit: ordinary counter counts cannot represent negative quantities. No signed extension is planned.

## Subsequent prompts (one at a time)
After a proposed removal: “Why does removing counters from just one side change whether the totals are equal?”
After equality justification: “How can you use three equal bags to find the number in one bag?”
Then: “How could you check your number in the original picture?”

## Tutor-only worked key
3x + 6 = 18: three equal groups and six loose counters equal eighteen counters.
Subtract six from each equal total: (3x + 6) - 6 = 18 - 6, so 3x = 12. Equal numbers minus the same number remain equal. Removing six from only one total makes it six smaller than the other, so it no longer describes equal totals.
Divide both totals by nonzero 3: x = 4. Twelve counters shared equally among three bags gives four per bag; equal numbers divided by the same nonzero number remain equal.
Check: 3 times 4 + 6 = 12 + 6 = 18.
Alternative valid division-first route: (3x + 6)/3 = 18/3 gives x + 2 = 6, then subtract 2 on each side to obtain x = 4. Division acts on the whole total, including the loose counters.

## Hint ladder
1. Notice the six loose counters outside the bags.
2. What operation would remove those loose counters?
3. If you remove six from the left total, what change keeps the right total equal?
4. Offer 3x = 18 - 6 and ask the learner to finish.
If 18/3 - 6 is proposed, ask how many loose counters belong in each share when the whole left total is divided into three equal shares.
Carry any help history forward for this problem.

## Unassisted transfer
“Two equal bags and four loose counters total fourteen: 2y + 4 = 14. Find the number in one bag and explain why your changes keep the totals equal.”
Tutor key: subtract four from each equal total to obtain 2y = 10; divide both by nonzero two to get y = 5. Check 2 times 5 + 4 = 14. No hints initially; record any later help.
