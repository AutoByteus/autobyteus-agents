## Goal, premises, and scope
Connect splitting a rectangle to distribution and justified equation steps. User reports knowing rectangle area rule; this is a rehearsal premise, not tested knowledge. Multiplication and division assumed from supplied context. x is the positive numerical measure of the left width in centimetres (not a counter count). Entire width is (x + 2) cm, height 3 cm, area 21 cm².

## Learner-facing opening and only visual asset
Text diagram, schematic/not to scale:

              x cm             2 cm
       +------------------+----------+
 3 cm  |    left part     |right part|
       +------------------+----------+
             total area = 21 cm²

Verbal alternative: A rectangle 3 cm high is split vertically into a left part x cm wide and a right part 2 cm wide. Both parts have the same 3 cm height. Together their area is 21 cm². Here x > 0.
Opening question: “How could you express the whole rectangle's area using its full width, and also using the two parts? Explain what the factors in your expressions mean.”
Show diagram and verbal alternative, then opening; do not prefill area expressions or solve. Wait for response.

## Subsequent prompts, one at a time
- Why does adding these two areas give the area of the whole rectangle?
- What area remains after removing the right strip, and how does the shared height help you find x?
- How can you check your width in the original rectangle?
If the user solves by dividing total area by height first, accept a valid route and return to explaining why the two part areas sum to the whole; do not insist on subtraction first.

## Tutor-only key with units and reasons
Numerical area in cm²: 3(x + 2) = 21, height times full width.
Parts: 3x cm² and (3 times 2) cm² = 6 cm². Nonoverlapping pieces cover the entire rectangle without gaps, so 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6 and 3x + 6 = 21. Splitting preserves total area.
Subtract 6 from both equal numerical area totals: 3x = 15. Physical interpretation: remove the right strip's 6 cm² from the whole 21 cm².
Divide both sides by nonzero 3: x = 5. Unit interpretation: 15 cm² / 3 cm = 5 cm.
Check: full width (5 + 2) cm = 7 cm; 3 cm times 7 cm = 21 cm².
Alternative: divide 3(x + 2) = 21 by nonzero 3, obtaining x + 2 = 7; subtract 2 from both equal width measures, giving x = 5. Still ask why the split expression agrees.
Lengths are in cm, areas in cm². Perimeter sums boundary lengths, not the areas of the two regions.
Model limits: positive lengths only for the two nondegenerate regions. Diagram shape/scale is not evidence. No negative-length argument or all-real-number proof is claimed.

## Hint ladder, smallest useful first
1. Notice that both parts share the 3 cm height.
2. What area does the 3 cm by 2 cm right part have?
3. Whole area equals left area plus right area: use the rectangle area rule for each part.
4. Offer 3(x + 2) = 3x + 3 times 2, and ask how each product maps to the picture.
If 3x + 2 is proposed: acknowledge the left-part product, then ask whether 2 cm is a length or an area and how much area a 3 cm by 2 cm strip has. Record all help; later correction on this same problem remains prompted.

## Fresh transfer, no hints initially
“A rectangle is 4 cm high and (y + 1) cm wide, with area 28 cm². Here y > 0. Draw or describe a split, write an equation, and find y, explaining why the split preserves area. Check your answer.”
Verbal model: left width y cm, right width 1 cm, both heights 4 cm, parts cover the whole.
Tutor key: 4(y + 1) = 28; left area 4y cm², right area 4 cm²; 4y + 4 = 28 since pieces cover the whole without overlap; subtract four from both equal area measures to give 4y = 24, divide by nonzero four to get y = 6. Left width 6 cm, full width 7 cm; check 4 times (6 + 1) = 28 cm². Distribution and equality reasons required, not just the number.
